What a private Egypt journey costs

An honest answer, written for travellers at the top of the market rather than the bottom — where the money goes, what moves the number, and where we sit.

How much does a private Egypt tour cost?

A private, tailor-made Egypt tour with a licensed Egyptologist guide, a private vehicle and good hotels costs roughly €200–450 per person per day. Kemet's own journeys run from €57 per person for a single day to €2,728 for a fourteen-day grand tour, and the per-person price falls as the party grows.

01 · The market

Four ways to see Egypt, and what each costs

The gap between the bottom and the top of the Egyptian market is wider than almost anywhere else in the world. Knowing which band you are shopping in is more useful than any single number.

Independent travel

€40–90 per person, per day

Public transport, budget hotels, self-guided sites, group day-tours bought locally. Egypt is genuinely rewarding at this level and many people travel it happily this way. It is not what we do, and we would not pretend otherwise.

Group tours

€90–180 per person, per day

A seat on a coach with twenty to forty others, fixed dates, a shared guide and an itinerary you cannot change. Good value if the itinerary happens to suit you, and the standard way most of the world sees Egypt.

Where Kemet sits

Private, tailor-made

€200–450 per person, per day

Where Kemet sits. Your own licensed Egyptologist, your own vehicle and driver, hotels chosen for the room rather than the star rating, and an itinerary written for you. Kemet's own multi-day journeys work out at €155–207 per person per day, at the entry to this band rather than the middle of it; the figure climbs with hotel tier and charter, and per-person cost falls as the party grows.

At the very top

€600+ per person, per day

Private charter between cities, a chartered dahabiya on the Nile, suites at the landmark hotels, private openings of the monuments. Kemet plans at this level too: the four Kemet Ultra journeys work out at €2,486–4,453 per person per day for a party of two, and are priced on their own page.

02 · The variables

Five things that move the number

In the order they matter, not the order people expect.

01

How many of you there are

This is the single largest factor and it surprises people. A private guide, a private vehicle and a private driver cost the same whether two people or six are using them — so the per-person price of a private journey falls sharply as the party grows. Two travellers carry the whole cost of the car; six split it. A family of six will often pay less per head for a fully private journey than a couple pays for the same itinerary.

02

When you come

Egypt has a genuine high season — roughly October to April, plus Christmas, New Year and Easter — and the difference is not marginal. The same Nile suite can cost twice as much over New Year as it does in early June. If your dates are flexible by even two weeks, late October, early March and the first half of June buy noticeably more journey for the same money.

03

Where you sleep

Hotels are the largest line in most quotes and the range in Egypt is enormous. A comfortable five-star in Cairo and a landmark hotel with a pyramid view are both 'five-star'; they are not the same price and they are not the same experience. On the Nile the gap is wider still — the difference between a standard cruise vessel and one of the small, restored boats is the difference between a holiday and a memory.

04

How you cross the country

Cairo to Luxor is an hour by air or ten by road. Aswan to Abu Simbel is a forty-minute flight or a three-hour desert drive each way. Domestic flights add cost and give back the thing you cannot buy more of, which is time on the ground. On a short trip they are almost always the right call.

05

How hard the days work

An itinerary with four sites a day is cheaper to build and worse to live. Slower days need more nights, and more nights cost more — but they are the difference between having seen Egypt and having been there. We would rather quote you seven unhurried days than five exhausting ones.

03 · The standard

What the money buys

Every operator in Egypt uses the word luxury. These are the specifics behind ours — the things you can check, before you pay for anything.

A licensed Egyptologist, not a guide

Every journey is led by a guide licensed by the Egyptian Ministry of Tourism with a degree in Egyptology. They are with you at the monuments, not waiting in the car park — and they are yours for the day, answering your questions rather than reciting to forty people.

Your party only

No shared coaches. No fixed departure dates. No strangers on your itinerary. If you are two people, the vehicle, the guide and the pace belong to two people.

Built around you, then written down

We design the itinerary from your interests and your dates — not from a catalogue page. You receive it in writing before anything is paid, and it changes as often as you want it to until it is right.

One price, itemised

The quote lists what is included and what is not, line by line, before you commit. Your Egyptologist, private air-conditioned transfers and lunch on touring days are inside the price on every journey, and entrance fees are inside it on every multi-day one. Where something sits outside — monument tickets on a day tour, for instance — it is named on the page you are reading, at the rate the ticket office charges, not discovered at the end.

Reachable while you travel

You have a direct line to the people who planned your journey for the whole time you are in Egypt — not a call centre, and not a number that stops answering after the deposit clears.

Time protected, not filled

A day with four sites crammed into it is a cheaper day to sell and a worse day to live. We plan around light, heat and crowds — the Valley of the Kings at opening, the Egyptian Museum when the coaches have gone — and we leave the afternoon alone.

Kemet sits at the upper end of the Egyptian market and we are comfortable saying so. A private Egyptologist for a week costs what it costs; so does a car that is yours alone and a room that faces the river rather than the car park. What we will not do is quote low, then recover the difference through optional extras, shopping stops and an itinerary that quietly turns into a group. Cost questions, answered

How much does a private tour of Egypt cost?

For a fully private, tailor-made journey with a licensed Egyptologist, a private vehicle and driver and good hotels, budget roughly €200–450 per person per day. Kemet's own journeys start from €57 per person for a single day and run to €2,728 per person for a fourteen-day grand tour. The per-person figure falls as the party grows, because the guide and the vehicle are shared between more people.

Why is a private Egypt tour more expensive than a group tour?

Because almost nothing is shared. A group tour divides one guide, one coach and one driver between thirty people; a private journey divides them between your party alone. You are also buying the ability to change the plan — to stay longer at Abu Simbel, to skip the papyrus shop, to start at six because the light is better. That flexibility has a real cost and it is the main thing you are paying for.

Is Egypt expensive to visit?

Egypt is one of the least expensive countries in the world for food, transport and entrance fees, and one where the gap between the bottom and the top of the market is unusually wide. You can see the Pyramids for the price of a museum ticket. What costs money is not access — it is the quality of the person standing next to you explaining what you are looking at, the car that collects you, and the room you come back to.

How much should I budget for a week in Egypt?

At the private, tailor-made level a week for two people typically lands between €2,800 and €6,000 in total, depending on hotels, season and whether you fly or drive between cities. That covers your Egyptologist throughout, all entrance fees, private transfers and lunch on touring days. International flights, the entry visa and gratuities sit outside it.

What is included in a Kemet price?

Your private licensed Egyptologist throughout, private air-conditioned transfers, hotel pickup and drop-off, lunch on each touring day and bottled water every day — plus all entrance fees on a multi-day journey. On a single day tour the price is quoted from a private party of six and monument tickets are paid at the published gate rate, so a government ticket never carries our markup. Excluded throughout: international flights, the Egypt entry visa, gratuities, personal expenses and named optional extras. Every quote itemises both lists before you commit to anything.

When do I pay?

25% of the total trip cost confirms your journey; the balance is due 30 days before departure. Nothing is paid before you have the written itinerary and the itemised price in front of you.

Are prices per person or for the whole party?

Every price on this site is per person, and every one is a starting point for a private journey — never a per-seat price on a shared departure. Because the fixed costs are split across your party, the per-person figure drops as more of you travel; tell us the party size and the quote reflects it.

Is it cheaper to book in Egypt when I arrive?

For a single day tour, sometimes. For anything else, no — and the saving is rarely what it appears. Booking on the ground means taking whichever guide is free rather than one chosen for your interests, competing for hotel rooms in high season, and losing the days you came for to arranging things. The travellers who do this most often end up paying a similar amount for a worse trip.
