The Sacred Senses
of Egypt

Incense burned since the pharaohs, the stones they held sacred, and the cotton and linen Egypt still grows — for a home that feels lighter, and a calmer you.

The ancient Egyptian word for incense is usually translated “that which makes divine.” Scent, stone and cloth were never only things here: they were how a person, a home and a temple were made ready.

I
Incense

The incense of the temples

Every morning the temples of Egypt woke to smoke. The priests burned incense as the sun crossed the sky — a ritual timed by the heavens, and one that never quite left Egyptian homes.

  1. DawnResinFrankincense as the sun rose.
  2. MiddayMyrrhAs the sun stood highest.
  3. SunsetKyphiA blend of sixteen ingredients, to send the day off.

The priests' day, as Plutarch records it in On Isis and Osiris. The recipe for kyphi is still carved on the walls of the laboratory at Edfu temple.

Six scents of an Egyptian home

Each as Cairo's incense sellers know it — where it comes from, and how it is used at home.

  • maja kuzmanovic · CC BY-SA 2.0

    Myrrhمُرّ

    Resin of the Commiphora tree, brought from the lands the Egyptians called Punt. The priests burned it at midday, and it was among the precious materials of embalming.

    At homeWarm, bitter-sweet and grounding — burned to settle a house at the end of the day.

  • WIPO · CC BY 2.0

    Luban dakarلبان دكر

    Frankincense of the finest grade — pale, clear tears of Boswellia resin, the kind Cairo's shops call dakar. Hatshepsut sent ships to Punt for living incense trees; the voyage is carved on her temple at Deir el-Bahari.

    At homeBright, clean and uplifting — the classic morning incense, burned to clear a room.

  • صندل

    Sandalwoodصندل

    A fragrant wood that came to Egypt along the trade routes from India, and is now the soft, creamy heart of many Cairo blends.

    At homeCalm and enveloping — an evening scent, for slowing down.

  • عود

    Oudعود

    Agarwood, prized across Egypt and the Arab world for centuries, and still the most precious thing on the incense shelf.

    At homeDeep, rich and lasting — burned to welcome guests, on Fridays and on feast days. A little perfumes a whole house.

  • نعناع

    Mintنعناع

    Grown along the Nile and in every Egyptian kitchen garden; dried leaves laid on the coals, or a few drops of its oil.

    At homeFresh and cooling — a lift for a heavy afternoon.

  • كافور

    Camphorكافور

    Crystals from the camphor tree, long kept in Egyptian homes and tucked into linen chests.

    At homeCool and sharp — used to freshen a room and keep linen fresh.

In Egyptian homes to this day, incense is burned after a guest has gone, before a new beginning, and against hasad — the evil eye.

These beliefs are older than any of us, and we share them as Egyptians live them. Whatever you believe, the ritual does what rituals do: it slows the hour down, and leaves a room feeling cared for.

How to burn it

  1. 1Light a charcoal disc in a heatproof dish, and wait until it turns grey.
  2. 2Lay a pea-sized piece of resin, wood or blend on top.
  3. 3Open a window. A little smoke goes a long way — incense should scent a room, not fill it.

II
Stones

Stones the pharaohs held sacred

To the ancient Egyptians a stone's colour was its power. Green and turquoise were new life, red was blood and vitality, deep blue was the night sky. They set them into amulets and collars, buried them with their kings — and Egyptians still wear them for the same reasons.

Right: the pectoral of Princess Sithathoryunet — gold, turquoise, lapis lazuli and carnelian, made about 3,900 years ago. The Metropolitan Museum of Art.

    mefkat · ancient Egyptian

    Joy, new life and protection.

    Lapis lazuli

    Carnelian

    herset · ancient Egyptian

    A red stone gathered in Egypt's deserts and carved into amulets for the living and the dead.

    Life, blood and vitality.

    Peridot

    zabargad · Arabic

    From Zabargad, an island in the Red Sea mined since antiquity — the source of some of the finest peridot ever found.

    Light, and the green of the growing season.

    hesmen · ancient Egyptian

    Mined at Wadi el-Hudi near Aswan in the Middle Kingdom, and strung into the jewellery of royal women.

The blue bead against the evil eye, the carnelian ring, the scarab worn for luck — Egypt never stopped.

III
Cotton & linen

Cloth grown by the Nile

Egypt has dressed in linen for five thousand years. Its priests, Herodotus noticed, wore nothing else, and Tutankhamun was buried with chests of linen shirts and tunics. Today the same river grows some of the finest cotton in the world.

Left: an Egyptian linen sheet woven about 3,500 years ago, its fringe still soft. The Metropolitan Museum of Art.

Egyptian cotton قطن

Egyptian cotton is extra-long staple: each fibre is longer than ordinary cotton's, so it spins into a finer, stronger, softer yarn — one that grows softer with every wash. It has been grown in the Nile Delta since the 1820s, when Muhammad Ali planted it for the world.

Johan Neven · CC BY 2.0

Egyptian linen كتان

Spun from flax grown by the Nile, linen is cool, strong and better with age. It breathes in the heat, softens as it is worn, and lasts for years — the cloth Egypt chose for its priests and its kings.

Natural — and still elegant

Pure cotton and linen against the skin, with nothing synthetic in between: the closest a garment gets to nature. Cut in modern, comfortable lines, so you never trade elegance for comfort.

Asked about incense, stones and cotton

What incense did the ancient Egyptians burn?

Resins above all — frankincense and myrrh, brought from the lands they called Punt — and kyphi, a blend of resins, honey, wine and spices whose recipe is carved on the walls of Edfu temple. Plutarch records the temple day: resin at dawn, myrrh at midday, kyphi at sunset.

What is luban dakar?

The name Cairo's incense sellers give the finest grade of frankincense: pale, clear tears of Boswellia resin. It burns bright and clean, and is the classic incense for the morning.

How do I burn resin incense at home?

Light a charcoal disc in a heatproof dish and let it turn grey, then lay a pea-sized piece of resin on top. Open a window: a little smoke goes a long way, and incense is meant to scent a room, not fill it.

Is incense used against the evil eye in Egypt?

Yes — it is one of the oldest traditions in Egyptian homes: incense is burned after a visit, before a new beginning and against hasad, the evil eye. It is a belief rather than a science, and we share it as Egyptians live it.

Which gemstones come from Egypt itself?

Turquoise from Sinai, peridot from Zabargad island in the Red Sea, amethyst from Wadi el-Hudi near Aswan, and carnelian from the deserts. Lapis lazuli, the deep blue of Tutankhamun's mask, was always imported, from the mountains of Afghanistan.

What makes Egyptian cotton different?

Its staple — the length of each fibre. Egyptian cotton is extra-long staple, so it spins into a finer, stronger and softer yarn, which grows softer with every wash.
