I

Incense

## The incense of the temples

Every morning the temples of Egypt woke to smoke. The priests burned incense as the sun crossed the sky — a ritual timed by the heavens, and one that never quite left Egyptian homes.

1. Dawn **Resin** Frankincense as the sun rose.
2. Midday **Myrrh** As the sun stood highest.
3. Sunset **Kyphi** A blend of sixteen ingredients, to send the day off.

The priests' day, as Plutarch records it in *On Isis and Osiris*. The recipe for kyphi is still carved on the walls of the laboratory at Edfu temple.

### Six scents of an Egyptian home

Each as Cairo's incense sellers know it — where it comes from, and how it is used at home.

- #### Myrrh مُرّ
  
  Resin of the Commiphora tree, brought from the lands the Egyptians called Punt. The priests burned it at midday, and it was among the precious materials of embalming.
  
  **At home** Warm, bitter-sweet and grounding — burned to settle a house at the end of the day.
- #### Luban dakar لبان دكر
  
  Frankincense of the finest grade — pale, clear tears of Boswellia resin, the kind Cairo's shops call dakar. Hatshepsut sent ships to Punt for living incense trees; the voyage is carved on her temple at Deir el-Bahari.
  
  **At home** Bright, clean and uplifting — the classic morning incense, burned to clear a room.
- #### Sandalwood صندل
  
  A fragrant wood that came to Egypt along the trade routes from India, and is now the soft, creamy heart of many Cairo blends.
  
  **At home** Calm and enveloping — an evening scent, for slowing down.
- #### Oud عود
  
  Agarwood, prized across Egypt and the Arab world for centuries, and still the most precious thing on the incense shelf.
  
  **At home** Deep, rich and lasting — burned to welcome guests, on Fridays and on feast days. A little perfumes a whole house.
- #### Mint نعناع
  
  Grown along the Nile and in every Egyptian kitchen garden; dried leaves laid on the coals, or a few drops of its oil.
  
  **At home** Fresh and cooling — a lift for a heavy afternoon.
- #### Camphor كافور
  
  Crystals from the camphor tree, long kept in Egyptian homes and tucked into linen chests.
  
  **At home** Cool and sharp — used to freshen a room and keep linen fresh.

> In Egyptian homes to this day, incense is burned after a guest has gone, before a new beginning, and against *hasad* — the evil eye.

These beliefs are older than any of us, and we share them as Egyptians live them. Whatever you believe, the ritual does what rituals do: it slows the hour down, and leaves a room feeling cared for.

### How to burn it

1. 1 Light a charcoal disc in a heatproof dish, and wait until it turns grey.
2. 2 Lay a pea-sized piece of resin, wood or blend on top.
3. 3 Open a window. A little smoke goes a long way — incense should scent a room, not fill it.

Oils

## Oils of the temples and the Delta

Egypt's perfumers were famous across the ancient world. They steeped flowers, resins and spices in oil to make perfumes and ointments, and the temples kept seven sacred oils for their rituals — at Edfu, the recipes for ointments are carved beside those for incense.

The craft never left. The Nile Delta still grows jasmine picked by hand before dawn, and small presses still press sesame and black seed cold, the way they always have.

Left: a perfume bottle of blue glass, made in Egypt about 3,300 years ago. The Metropolitan Museum of Art.

**The seven sacred oils.** An offering slab carved for Ankhwadjes in the Old Kingdom: each hollow held one of the oils named above it. The Metropolitan Museum of Art.

### Seven oils of Egypt

For scent, for the skin and for massage — each pressed or distilled the way Egypt has always done it.

- #### Jasmine ياسمين
  
  Egypt is one of the world's great growers of jasmine. In the Nile Delta the flowers are picked by hand before sunrise, while their scent is strongest, and extracted the same day.
  
  **Use** Warm, heady and joyful — a drop in a carrier oil for the evening.
- #### Geranium عطرة
  
  Grown in Middle Egypt and distilled for perfumers across the world, Egyptian geranium is prized for its rose-like scent.
  
  **Use** Bright and rosy — a balancing note in a massage blend.
- #### Chamomile بابونج
  
  Egypt is one of the world's largest growers of chamomile, in the fields of Fayoum and Beni Suef.
  
  **Use** Soft and apple-sweet — for winding down at night.
- #### Black seed حبة البركة
  
  Pressed cold from the seeds of Nigella. Black seed was found in the tomb of Tutankhamun.
  
  **Use** Rich and peppery — used on skin and hair in Egyptian homes for generations.
- #### Sesame سيرج
  
  The everyday oil of Egypt, pressed from sesame seed in small traditional presses.
  
  **Use** Light and warming — a classic base for massage.
- #### Moringa زيت البان
  
  Ben oil, from the moringa tree. Ancient perfumers prized it because it keeps for years without turning and carries a scent well.
  
  **Use** Silky and almost scentless — the base that carries another oil.
- #### Blue lotus لوتس أزرق
  
  The sacred flower of the sun, painted on temple walls and carried in the hands of queens. Its scent is rare and precious.
  
  **Use** Delicate and floral — for meditation and quiet.

### A massage, the Egyptian way

A massage oil is simple: a carrier — sesame, sweet almond or moringa — and a few drops of an essential oil such as jasmine, geranium or chamomile. Warm it between your palms, breathe it in, and work slowly.

Essential oils are strong. Always dilute them in a carrier oil, test on a small patch of skin first, keep them away from the eyes, and ask your doctor before using them if you are pregnant.

Stones

## Stones the pharaohs held sacred

To the ancient Egyptians a stone's colour was its power. Green and turquoise were new life, red was blood and vitality, deep blue was the night sky. They set them into amulets and collars, buried them with their kings — and Egyptians still wear them for the same reasons.

Right: the pectoral of Princess Sithathoryunet — gold, turquoise, lapis lazuli and carnelian, made about 3,900 years ago. The Metropolitan Museum of Art.

- ### Turquoise
  
  *mefkat* · ancient Egyptian
  
  Mined in Sinai since the Old Kingdom, at Serabit el-Khadim and Wadi Maghara — where a temple stands to Hathor, Lady of Turquoise.
  
  Joy, new life and protection.
- ### Lapis lazuli
  
  *khesbed* · ancient Egyptian
  
  Brought from the mountains of Afghanistan for the hair of the gods and the night sky inlaid on Tutankhamun's mask.
  
  The heavens, and truth.
- ### Carnelian
  
  *herset* · ancient Egyptian
  
  A red stone gathered in Egypt's deserts and carved into amulets for the living and the dead.
  
  Life, blood and vitality.
- ### Peridot
  
  *zabargad* · Arabic
  
  From Zabargad, an island in the Red Sea mined since antiquity — the source of some of the finest peridot ever found.
  
  Light, and the green of the growing season.
- ### Amethyst
  
  *hesmen* · ancient Egyptian
  
  Mined at Wadi el-Hudi near Aswan in the Middle Kingdom, and strung into the jewellery of royal women.
  
  Calm, and a clear head.
- ### Garnet
  
  *granatum* · Latin
  
  Strung into Egyptian necklaces since before the pharaohs — garnet beads lie in Predynastic graves — and set in the jewellery of the Middle Kingdom. Its name comes from the pomegranate.
  
  The red of life, like carnelian.

A princess's girdle and bracelets — gold, carnelian, turquoise and lapis lazuli — from the reign of Amenemhat III. The Metropolitan Museum of Art.

Cotton & linen

## Cloth grown by the Nile

Egypt has dressed in linen for five thousand years. Its priests, Herodotus noticed, wore nothing else, and Tutankhamun was buried with chests of linen shirts and tunics. Today the same river grows some of the finest cotton in the world.

Left: an Egyptian linen sheet woven about 3,500 years ago, its fringe still soft. The Metropolitan Museum of Art.

### Egyptian cotton قطن

Egyptian cotton is **extra-long staple**: each fibre is longer than ordinary cotton's, so it spins into a finer, stronger, softer yarn — one that grows softer with every wash. It has been grown in the Nile Delta since the 1820s, when Muhammad Ali planted it for the world.

### Egyptian linen كتان

Spun from flax grown by the Nile, linen is cool, strong and better with age. It breathes in the heat, softens as it is worn, and lasts for years — the cloth Egypt chose for its priests and its kings.

### Natural — and still elegant

## Asked about incense, oils, stones and cotton

### What incense did the ancient Egyptians burn?

Resins above all — frankincense and myrrh, brought from the lands they called Punt — and kyphi, a blend of resins, honey, wine and spices whose recipe is carved on the walls of Edfu temple. Plutarch records the temple day: resin at dawn, myrrh at midday, kyphi at sunset.

### What is luban dakar?

The name Cairo's incense sellers give the finest grade of frankincense: pale, clear tears of Boswellia resin. It burns bright and clean, and is the classic incense for the morning.

### How do I burn resin incense at home?

Light a charcoal disc in a heatproof dish and let it turn grey, then lay a pea-sized piece of resin on top. Open a window: a little smoke goes a long way, and incense is meant to scent a room, not fill it.

### Is incense used against the evil eye in Egypt?

Yes — it is one of the oldest traditions in Egyptian homes: incense is burned after a visit, before a new beginning and against hasad, the evil eye. It is a belief rather than a science, and we share it as Egyptians live it.

### What oils did the ancient Egyptians use?

Plant oils — moringa (ben oil), sesame and castor among them — steeped with flowers, resins and spices to make perfumes and ointments. Temples kept seven sacred oils for their rituals, and offering slabs with seven hollows, one for each oil, survive from the Old Kingdom.

### How do I make a massage oil?

Mix a few drops of an essential oil — jasmine, geranium or chamomile — into a carrier oil such as sesame, sweet almond or moringa. Essential oils are too strong to use on the skin undiluted: test the blend on a small patch of skin first, keep it away from the eyes, and ask your doctor before using essential oils if you are pregnant.

### Is Egyptian jasmine special?

Egypt is one of the world's largest producers of jasmine. In the Nile Delta the flowers are picked by hand before sunrise, when their scent is strongest, and extracted the same day.

### Which gemstones come from Egypt itself?

Turquoise from Sinai, peridot from Zabargad island in the Red Sea, amethyst from Wadi el-Hudi near Aswan, and carnelian from the deserts. Lapis lazuli, the deep blue of Tutankhamun's mask, was always imported, from the mountains of Afghanistan.

### What makes Egyptian cotton different?

Its staple — the length of each fibre. Egyptian cotton is extra-long staple, so it spins into a finer, stronger and softer yarn, which grows softer with every wash.
