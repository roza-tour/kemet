# Egypt in November

Ideal weather, rising demand, and the start of the winter season.

Cairo **23–26°C**

Luxor **29–31°C**

Aswan **30–32°C**

Red Sea air **27–29°C**

Sea temp **25–26°C**

Crowds **Busy**

Prices **High**

Typical daytime highs, rounded. A planning aid, not a forecast — crowd and price levels are relative to Egypt's own year rather than to anywhere else.

November is when the world remembers Egypt exists. The weather is close to flawless — high twenties in Luxor, mid-twenties in Cairo, dry and clear — and the international season begins in earnest. It is a superb month to travel and the last one before the Christmas and New Year peak, which starts around 20 December.

## What November is good for

- Sightseeing at any hour: the first month since March where midday at Karnak is comfortable
- Nile cruising in near-perfect conditions
- The Red Sea still warm enough for easy swimming

## What to be realistic about

- Demand rises sharply through the month — the good Nile boats and hotel rooms go early
- Prices climb steadily through the month as the season fills
- Evenings turn cool, especially on the water

## Occasions this month

The start of Egypt's main international season. Nothing fixed, which is part of its appeal.

## Our honest verdict

The first three weeks of November are excellent and slightly under-appreciated. Book early — this is the month that sells out quietly.

## Egypt in November — questions

### Is November a good month to visit Egypt?

Very. Temperatures of 29–31°C in Luxor and the mid-twenties in Cairo are close to ideal for sightseeing, the air is dry and clear, and the Red Sea is still warm. It is high season, so book well ahead — particularly for Nile boats.

### How far ahead should I book for November?

Three to four months for hotels and standard cruise vessels; six if you want a specific dahabiya or a landmark hotel room. November through February is Egypt's main season and the best accommodation is finite.

### Do I need warm clothes in Egypt in November?

A light jacket or a warm layer for evenings, yes — particularly on a Nile boat, where the temperature drops noticeably after sunset and the deck is exposed. Days are warm enough for short sleeves throughout.
